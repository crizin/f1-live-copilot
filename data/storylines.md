# 2026 Season Storylines

> Things Claude might not know from training data — recent drama, memes, narratives.
> Updated throughout the season as stories develop.
> Championship points live in `standings.md`. This file carries the narrative, not the table.

Last update: 2026-11-09, the day after the São Paulo Grand Prix (raced November 8).
Las Vegas is next, then Qatar and the Abu Dhabi finale.

**Where it stands:** **Both titles are settled, and both went to Mercedes.** Kimi Antonelli
clinched the drivers' title in Mexico City with four rounds to run. At 20 years and 68 days
he's the youngest champion in F1 history, and Italy's first since Ascari in 1953. A week
later Russell P4 and Antonelli P5 at Interlagos sealed Mercedes' ninth constructors' title
and their first since 2021. Norris won a wet-dry São Paulo from pole, and Verstappen came
from 21st on the grid to second. Three races to go. Verstappen went into Brazil second in
the drivers' standings, two points ahead of Hamilton, and outscored him there (P2 to P9).

## The Big Stories

### The new rules reshuffled the grid

The 2026 power unit overhaul — electric power tripled, MGU-H gone, 100% sustainable fuel
— redrew the order. Where each team landed:

- **Mercedes**: Got it right on pace — Russell's Baku pole was worth eight tenths over the
  next car. Double champions anyway: the constructors' title came at Interlagos, their
  ninth, which ties Williams for third all-time. But reliability has bitten all year. Russell's PU let go while leading at
  Montreal, Antonelli's died four laps from home at Barcelona while running second, at Spa an
  energy-deployment glitch cut Russell's power through Eau Rouge and pitched him into a
  race-ending lap-1 clash with Hamilton — and at Sepang Russell's PU failed behind the late
  safety car, running third. Wolff: "I'm so massively annoyed... one of my worst
  experiences, to be honest." On the earlier failures, James
  Allison said the first two failures "originate in the same broad part of the battery"; the
  fix is phasing new modules into the fleet over the season, not an overnight cure. Wolff on
  the cost: "we just can't compete for a championship if every second race a car is losing
  fat points." The bill keeps coming due in grid penalties: 30 places for Antonelli at
  Monza (he won anyway), and Russell from 19th in Singapore, where ninth was the best he
  could salvage.
- **McLaren**: The upgrade package at Hungary turned their season — first win of the year
  straight after it, then Norris won Zandvoort too off a fiddly damp-to-dry race he rated
  "probably one of my better wins." At Interlagos Norris won from pole by 14.6s for his
  third win of the year and second straight in Brazil, after Antonelli spun out of the
  lead. Piastri was sixth. Monza was the reality check: Verstappen held both of them off for P3 in a
  straight-line-speed contest while they squabbled over P4. Madrid was crueller — pole,
  race controlled, then lost to VSC timing and a 7s front-right. Baku was worst of all:
  Norris wiped out in the Colapinto pile-up, Piastri locking himself out of podium
  contention, and a car with fundamental limitations around the place. Stella was blunt
  about it — "we were not fast enough in the corners but at the same time, and mainly, we
  were not fast enough in the straights" — and put the straight-line problem down to gear
  ratio choices versus the works Mercedes team, a draggier car, and a power unit that "was
  not optimised."
- **Red Bull-Ford**: The in-house PU is a work in progress, trending up. A big Austria
  upgrade package put Verstappen on the podium there, but the story has been the
  "Macarena" revolving rear wing — a trick Ferrari pioneered and Red Bull adapted, run
  since Miami. It failed to close properly twice, dumping rear downforce mid-corner and
  binning Verstappen in Austria qualifying and again at Stowe while he ran P3 four laps
  from the end at Silverstone. "It's getting very dangerous for me," he said; the team
  parked it from Spa and reverted to the old conventional spec, a compromise he called
  "slower" but worth it. The real deficit isn't the combustion engine — it's energy
  deployment, which the FIA's ADUO ranking doesn't measure at all: it scores the combustion
  side only, has Red Bull as the benchmark, and therefore locks them out of upgrading the
  electrical side they actually need. Verstappen, drily, on being told again that he has the
  best engine: "apparently the fastest engine." The trajectory kept climbing — P3 at Monza,
  P2 at Madrid and Baku — and then it finally paid. Sepang: first pole and first win of
  the year. Singapore: the full sweep, both poles and both wins, his first ever at Marina
  Bay. "It's taken a while, but I think this race was very good... The last two races have
  been really positive for us." Austin cooled it a little — a poor start, a car that was
  hard to drive — but he still dragged it to third, and then Mexico City brought another
  win, his sixth there: from fifth on the grid, through the Ferrari mess and a red-flag
  restart, holding Hamilton off by eight tenths. "They should actually call it the 'Maxico'
  Grand Prix." In Brazil an extra power unit dropped him to 21st on the grid, and he went
  through the field in the wet to finish second. Hadjar was told to let him by for P2 and
  took third, so Red Bull had both cars on the podium.
- **Aston Martin-Honda**: Mexico summed it up — both cars out with engine failures within
  seconds of each other, early in the race. Honda cited cooling; Alonso suggested Honda had
  underestimated Mexico's altitude.
- **Audi**: First season with its own PU, and reliability still bites — Hulkenberg's car
  caught fire on the reconnaissance lap to the Miami sprint grid. They're also the team
  currently picking a fight with the stewards over one point — see the Tsunoda appeal
  under Monza. Singapore was another rough one: Bortoleto's car caught fire after an
  apparent gearbox problem, though Hulkenberg scored P8.
- **Cadillac**: The new 11th team, still backmarkers but operating better every weekend, and
  still hunting a first point. Monaco was the gut-punch: Perez crossed the line inside the
  top ten, then copped a 10-second post-race penalty for being out of position at the
  restart and dropped to last. Mexico was scarier: Bottas had a front-left suspension
  failure at high speed and hit the Turn 1 barriers. He walked to the ambulance and was
  released. Budkowski: "We will fully investigate the cause of this failure and ensure such
  accidents do not happen again."

### The Madring's debut went badly

F1's newest circuit — a part-street, part-purpose-built lap around the IFEMA exhibition
grounds in Madrid — produced four on-track overtakes after lap one. For scale, Monza
managed 78. Bortoleto: "I almost fell asleep." Norris: "I don't know how someone looks at
this design and goes, mint!" Alonso on La Monumental, the banked showpiece: "the driver
input is just nothing." Spain's transport minister called it "an absolute dud." Layout
changes (Turns 18-19, the Turn 5 chicane, the split pit lane) are expected for next year;
Madrid is on a 10-year deal, and this was race one.

### "Super clipping" — the buzzword of 2026

With the new ~50/50 ICE/electric split, cars run out of battery before the end of long
straights and visibly slow — sometimes by 50 km/h — while drivers are still flat on the
throttle. Norris said the cars are "probably the worst ever made." Hamilton called it
"the least enjoyable part" of the new rules. Alonso went furthest — "probably the worst
generation of cars I ever drove," and flatly, "hybrid cars should not be racing." Monaco
got a one-off mandatory engine map plus a ban on active-aero "straight mode," the first
circuit-specific PU mode ever mandated.

### Bearman's 50G crash and the rule tweaks it triggered

Ollie Bearman crashed at Suzuka's Spoon at 191 mph after closing on Colapinto's Alpine
roughly 50 km/h faster — a textbook super-clipping speed delta. He walked away with a
bruised knee, but the crash forced mid-season energy-management "refinements" from Miami
on (less qualifying harvest, capped deployment and boost). Verstappen called them "a
tickle."

The bigger lever landed around Barcelona: F1 agreed to shift the ICE/electric balance away
from 50/50 — to 58/42 in 2027 and 60/40 in 2028. That 60/40 is the exact ratio Verstappen
had named as his minimum to stay in the sport, and he cautiously approved: "the changes they
are making are heading into the right direction." The super-clipping era has a sunset date
now, even if the 2026 cars are stuck with it.

### Antonelli — the youngest world champion ever

Kimi Antonelli clinched the title in Mexico City on November 1 with a third place and four
rounds to spare. Aged 20 years and 68 days, he beat Vettel's record (23, in 2010) by more
than three years, and he's Italy's first champion since Alberto Ascari in 1953. He's one of
only six drivers to win the title in the same season as their first Grand Prix win, and the
first to do it in car number 12 since Senna in 1988. He even passed Leclerc on the inside
late on to take the podium on the road. "I'm speechless. I'm trying to process what has just
happened." On the last lap he backed off because he couldn't see the track: "I lost probably
two seconds because I was already crying, basically." Vettel sent his congratulations in an
Instagram video: "Only very few of us manage to achieve that, and nobody has done it as young
as you."

Nine wins got him here. He became the youngest championship leader ever back at Suzuka, at
19, and from then on the things that slowed him down were his own car and the stewards, not
a rival on track: the Barcelona PU failure four laps from the flag while running second, and
a bizarre left-front wheel-shield failure at Silverstone that turned a near-certain win into
16th. Baku was the first mistake that was his own (into the wall in Q1, P5 from 16th), and
then Sepang and Singapore effectively settled it. Told in Singapore he could clinch in
Austin, he seemed genuinely surprised: "Can I? Really?" He then won Austin from pole and
still had to wait one more race. His one recurring flaw is track limits. A Turn 6 violation
in Sepang Q2 cost him a set of softs, and in Singapore he collected enough warnings for a
black-and-white flag.

Monza is the one people will remember. Now 20, he took a 30-place PU penalty at his home
race, started 19th on hards, gambled on mediums at the lap-2 restart while most went hard,
and led inside 18 laps. Brundle called it one of the best races he'd commentated on in 30
years. First Italian to win at Monza since Ludovico Scarfiotti in 1966 — in a Mercedes,
which the tifosi seemed entirely fine with once the noise started. Madrid was the
inherited kind, and he said so: "I think Lando today deserved to win."

The clearest sign of where he stands isn't any single result: Mercedes ordered Russell to
move over for him at Zandvoort, then split the strategy his way at Monza (see Rivalries).
That team picked its horse a while ago.

### Verstappen re-signed — and the 2027 grid is filling in

He's staying, through the end of 2030, announced on the Thursday of the Dutch GP weekend.
The story that dominated half a season — the performance exit clause, the open talk of
retiring because he wasn't enjoying the 2026 cars, the real talks his management held with
McLaren — resolved in a press release. Two details still matter: the exit clause survives
into the new deal ("that stays the same"), and he's negotiated permission to race endurance
events, 24-hour races included. His race engineer of a decade, Gianpiero Lambiase, still
leaves for McLaren — but in 2028. Carlos Sainz re-signed with Williams on multi-year terms
the day before, despite a miserable 2026 in an FW48 that has him nowhere.

**Red Bull's second seat is settled.** Hadjar has been extended for 2027, announced on the
Wednesday of the Baku weekend, ahead of his return from injury — remarkable job security by
the standards of that car, where nobody has lasted long next to Verstappen. Mekies called it
"an easy decision." He repaid it with a Baku podium on his return. Liam Lawson, who scored
in the Red Bull while Hadjar sat out a wrist fracture, goes back to Racing Bulls.

**Câmara replaces Ocon at Haas, and he started early.** Ocon announced his own exit on
September 30 ("This is absolutely not the end of my Formula 1 career"), and on October 15
Haas signed the 21-year-old Brazilian Rafael Câmara — F2 championship leader, Ferrari
junior, same pipeline as Bearman — for 2027. Komatsu: "such a young, amazing talent."
Bearman isn't officially confirmed but is widely expected to stay. After the Mexico blow-up
(below), Haas confirmed Ocon won't race for them again in 2026 and brought Câmara's debut
forward to his home race at Interlagos. He finished 20th, two laps down. Haas hasn't said
who drives the last three rounds.

**Ocon's exit got ugly early.** Haas pulled him out of the Mexico City weekend after Friday
practice, with no reason given. On Friday he was unhappy about running mediums instead of
softs in FP2 ("don't do this to me" on the radio), and a heated discussion with Komatsu
followed that evening. Press reports said it turned physical. Ocon admitted "a difference in
viewpoints led to a discussion" but "categorically" rejected any suggestion of physical
violence. Komatsu would only say: "I can't tell you much but I can tell you he's not ill."
Reserve Ryo Hirakawa stepped in at 32, qualified 19th, and inherited tenth after penalties,
scoring a point on his debut. Ocon never came back, and he's a self-declared "free agent"
with no 2027 seat.

**Alonso isn't retiring.** Aston Martin confirmed an unchanged Alonso-Stroll line-up for
2027 on September 29. Alonso: "racing is my life, my passion, and I know I still have
the speed and determination to compete at the highest level."

That leaves Racing Bulls as the only team yet to confirm a 2027 line-up — Permane said in
Singapore they'd pick "in the coming weeks."

### Ferrari's year two — and the rules-of-engagement row

Ferrari's home weekend detonated: Hamilton and Leclerc touched at the first chicane on lap
1, Leclerc crashed out of the race on lap 2, and Ferrari was down to one car in front of
the tifosi before anyone had settled. Nobody was penalised (stewards: racing incident) and
the pundits split down the middle. Leclerc took it on the chin: "We went too far." Hamilton
made it about the team: "When I was at Mercedes, we had written rules of engagement...
Here, there are no rules. We need to address that." And then the sting — he thinks it's
already moot: "we just lost so much ground to them on points, and it's far too late for
those decisions to come."

Where it's landed: nowhere. Hamilton and Vasseur met between Monza and Madrid — Hamilton
called it positive, with "different opinions" — and Vasseur has publicly pushed back,
arguing rules of engagement already exist in some form ("to say there are no rules of
engagement does not reflect reality") and that "you cannot expect every possible scenario
you might encounter on track to be written into the contracts." Hamilton says it hasn't come
up again since. Since then Hamilton's strategy calls have been the theme. At Sepang he
talked the team out of pitting for inters and drove from 18th back to third. In Singapore
the same instinct bit him: Ferrari split strategies, Leclerc on softs and pitting under the
VSC, Hamilton staying out — and as his tyres went, Antonelli and both McLarens
came through, dropping him from a podium spot to sixth while Leclerc took second. "A
painful lesson today, but I take full responsibility for the decisions I made."

Then Mexico City dragged the rules-of-engagement row straight back up. Hamilton took his
first Ferrari pole (his first anywhere since Hungary 2023) and Ferrari ran 1-2. At Turn 4
on lap 1 the two of them touched again, Leclerc cut across the grass and picked up a
five-second penalty, and Verstappen took both of them for the win. Hamilton on the radio:
"Pushed out by my own teammate." Vasseur: "When you are 1-2 in Turn 4, you don't imagine
that you won't win the race." Hamilton still took second, while Leclerc came fourth.

The backdrop: year two at Maranello delivered the moment the whole move was for, Hamilton's
maiden Ferrari win at Barcelona, and Leclerc answered with a controlled Silverstone win.
The pecking order has changed hands all year without either of them landing a decisive
blow, which is precisely why the no-rules question got so loud at Monza.

### Things worth remembering about this calendar

**Bahrain and Saudi Arabia were both cancelled** — a first for the modern calendar,
geopolitical tensions forcing the FIA's hand — leaving a five-week gap between Suzuka and
Miami. Bahrain's slot got rehomed at Sepang as the "Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia," F1's
first visit since 2017 — and Verstappen, who won the last Malaysian GP, won this one too.

**Zandvoort is gone too.** The promoter chose not to continue past 2026, citing the
long-term financial risk of hosting without state subsidies; nobody has ruled out a return,
but there's no deal. A record 325,000 came across the three days of the farewell — and
Verstappen put it in the barrier at the last corner of lap one in front of all of them.

**Active aero arrived**: teams adjusting aero surfaces on the fly, DRS on steroids but with
defensive and cornering uses too. It has changed overtaking dynamics a lot, and some drivers
adapted faster than others.

## Rivalries to Watch

- **Antonelli vs Russell** (Mercedes): Over. Antonelli is champion, and it was never really
  a rivalry between equals: a number one and a driver who kept being asked to make way,
  politely. At Zandvoort Russell won the Sprint, then was told to let Antonelli by on Sunday.
  His radio was pure acid, "Is he fighting for the win? Or, is he planning to catch
  Norris?", before he cooled off and called it the right call. Monza did it again by
  stealth, when the VSC strategy split went Antonelli's way. Russell's own bid died on
  reliability. After his PU failed at Sepang: "You've almost got to laugh. I'm beyond
  disappointment." Mexico brought a lap-one penalty and a €5,000 fine for Mercedes when he
  skipped post-race media. Wolff: "There have been too many occasions this season where we
  have let George down." His consolation was sealing the constructors' title with P4 at
  Interlagos, despite a spin on pit exit.
- **Norris vs Piastri** (McLaren): Reigning champion vs the Australian who almost took the
  2025 title, and the "papaya rules" détente is fraying. Piastri hasn't been on the podium
  since Miami, while Norris has three wins. They collided at Turn 5 on the last lap of the
  Singapore Sprint, Norris spinning and Piastri out, and Norris insisted he "had the right."
  At Monza they swapped P4 wheel-to-wheel, Piastri putting Norris on the grass at Curva
  Grande, which Brundle called "personal." Hungary was the first blow-up: a strategy call
  handed Norris the win and Piastri went full ice on the radio ("Don't f****** talk to me")
  before his gearbox failed.
- **Hamilton vs Leclerc** (Ferrari): No longer just a pecking-order question. They
  collided on lap 1 at Monza, where Leclerc crashed out, and again at Turn 4 in Mexico,
  where it cost Ferrari a 1-2. "Pushed out by my own teammate." See "Ferrari's year two"
  above.
- **Norris vs Verstappen**: 2025 title rivals, now in different tiers — Verstappen came to
  Austin chasing a third straight win and still finished ahead of Norris there. Occasional
  flashes, nothing sustained.
- **Hamilton vs Verstappen**: dormant for years, awake again. Mexico had them nose to tail
  after the red-flag restart, Verstappen winning by 0.799s, and Sepang put them on the
  front row together, Verstappen on pole by 0.298s. Austria gave us "Clear
  penalty!" on the radio and Hamilton's "you don't expect to go around the outside of a
  champion" reply; Madrid gave us an early scrap over corner-cutting that both of them took
  to the radio, Hamilton wanting the stewards involved and Verstappen insisting Hamilton
  started it. Neither is in a title fight. Both are entirely willing anyway.

## Memes & Fan Culture

- **The Antonelli–Bono prank war.** Mercedes have leaned all the way in with videos of Kimi
  sabotaging Peter Bonnington (Hamilton's old engineer, now Antonelli's) — the one where he
  gets Bono's access card deactivated across Brackley HQ, leaving Bono muttering "the little
  s\*\*\* has done it again" at a red-flashing door, went everywhere.
- **"Concentrate on the driving, not on the radio moaning"** — Wolff to Antonelli mid-sprint
  at Montreal. Instant T-shirt material, re-cut over every petty workplace complaint on F1
  TikTok.
- **"Don't f****** talk to me"** — Piastri's ice-cold radio to his own McLaren pit wall
  at Hungary, paired with the earlier "Get out of the f****** way you idiot" at Sainz.
- **"Is he fighting for the win? Or, is he planning to catch Norris?"** — Russell's
  withering radio at Zandvoort on being told to let his own title rival past (see Rivalries).
- **"There are no rules"** — Hamilton on Ferrari's absent rules of engagement after the
  Monza clash, delivered with the weary precision of a man who has had this argument
  before. Immediately weaponised by both halves of the Ferrari fanbase against each other.
- **Monza's Italian winner.** First home win there in 60 years, from 19th, in front of a
  grandstand that turned up to watch Ferrari — and Ferrari had one car through the gravel and
  the other in the barrier before lap 3. The tifosi-cheering-a-Mercedes footage is the
  fan-edit fuel of the season.
- **"I almost fell asleep"** — Bortoleto reviewing the Madring's debut, and the line that
  stuck to it. A brand-new circuit getting dunked on by the drivers and the Spanish
  government inside the same 24 hours is not how a debut is supposed to go.
- **"That's not driving that deserves to be in Formula 1"** — Norris, straight out of the
  car at Baku, calling for a one-race ban for Colapinto. The clip did numbers, the replies
  did worse, and then, "with the benefit of two days," Norris messaged Colapinto to
  apologise and the whole thing reversed polarity. Both of them came out of it looking
  better than the internet did.
- **"Can I? Really?"**: Antonelli in Singapore, on being told he could wrap the title up in
  Austin. He couldn't, so it waited a week, and when it came he lost "probably two seconds"
  on the last lap crying in the car.
- **"Pushed out by my own teammate"**: Hamilton's radio in Mexico, after the Turn 4 clash
  with Leclerc that cost Ferrari a 1-2. It's the sequel to "There are no rules," and it's
  being used the same way.
- **"Maxico"**: "They should actually call it the 'Maxico' Grand Prix," said Verstappen
  after his sixth win there.
- **"I can tell you he's not ill"**: Komatsu's non-explanation for why Ocon vanished from
  the Haas garage between Friday and Saturday. The paddock has been filling in the gaps
  ever since.
- **Hamilton's first Ferrari win** was the season's big feel-good moment — an emotional
  maiden victory for the Scuderia and the first all-British podium since 1968.

## Recent Incidents & Controversies

### São Paulo GP (November 8) — Norris wins, Mercedes take the constructors'

- **Norris won from pole by 14.6s.** Antonelli, alongside him on the front row, passed him
  into Turn 1 and led until he reported "something felt wrong with the car" and spun at
  Turn 1 around lap 20. Norris led from there and was never threatened.
- **Weather made it a strategy race.** It started wet, the track was declared fully dry
  by lap 23, and late rain sent the field back onto inters. Norris and Verstappen both
  made their final stops on lap 63 of 71.
- **Verstappen from 21st to P2** after a grid penalty for an extra power unit. Red Bull told
  Hadjar to let him through around lap 38, and Hadjar held on for third.
- **Mercedes' title came from P4 and P5**: Russell recovered to fourth despite a spin on
  pit exit, and Antonelli to fifth after his spin.
- The rest of the points: Piastri P6, Leclerc P7, Lawson P8, Hamilton P9 (a lap down) and
  Hulkenberg P10. Câmara was 20th on debut. Stroll retired after 30 laps. Bottas
  wasn't classified; results tables differ on whether he started (DNS in one, DNF in another).

### Earlier in the season

The ones still worth knowing about:

- **Mexico City GP**: Antonelli's P3 clinched the title. Verstappen won from fifth after
  the Ferrari Turn 4 clash, then held off Hamilton by eight tenths after a 33-minute red
  flag. Bottas had a high-speed front-left suspension failure into the Turn 1 barriers and
  was OK. Both Aston Martins lost their Honda engines early, within seconds of each other.
  Russell got a 10-second penalty for a lap-1 clash with Hadjar, and Hirakawa scored a point
  on debut, subbing for Ocon.

- **United States GP** (Austin): Antonelli's one-stop win from pole, by 13s. It still left
  the title open for a week. Leclerc P2; Verstappen P3 in a car he called "undriveable,"
  after Red Bull ordered Hadjar to let him through.
- **Singapore GP** (Sprint weekend): Verstappen four for four, with Sprint pole, Sprint win,
  GP pole and GP win, the last by 7.3s from Leclerc and Antonelli. In the Sprint, Russell
  crashed on lap one and Norris and Piastri collided on the last lap. Two mechanical VSCs
  came in the GP, one of them Bortoleto's Audi catching fire.
- **Bahrain GP in Malaysia** (Sepang): Verstappen's first win of 2026, from his first pole;
  Hamilton third from 18th on a slicks gamble. The start was a mess, with torrential rain
  and then an "unprecedented" software glitch that cut several cars' power on the
  formation lap. Russell's PU died behind the late safety car.
- **Azerbaijan GP** (Baku): Russell by 0.196s from Verstappen, the closest finish since
  2002. Colapinto took out Gasly and Norris at a restart, and Norris called for a ban and
  then apologised (see Memes). His five-place grid drop reopened the argument over whether
  the FIA has gone too lenient.
- **Spanish GP** (Madrid): Norris on pole and leading until a badly timed VSC and a slow
  front-right stop; Antonelli won. The circuit became the story (see "The Madring's
  debut went badly").
- **Italian GP** (Monza): Antonelli from 19th to the win; Ferrari's home race was over in
  two laps. Audi's appeal over Tsunoda's P10 is still pending, with no hearing date
  reported.
- **Dutch GP**: Zandvoort's farewell. Verstappen hit the barrier on lap one, and Norris
  won. Russell won the Sprint and was then told to move over for Antonelli on Sunday.
- **FIA ADUO ruling** (still contested): Red Bull-Ford was ranked the best engine of 2026
  and locked out of catch-up upgrades. "We don't feel like we are the best."
- **Hungarian GP**: McLaren's first win, Norris's, along with Piastri's radio meltdown and
  gearbox DNF.
- **British GP**: Leclerc's first Silverstone win. Antonelli's wheel-shield failure, and
  Verstappen's second rear-wing failure in two weekends.
- **Austrian GP**: Russell's "cold-blooded" win with a failed drinks system. Rookie Lindblad
  ignored orders and passed Lawson anyway, and pundits keep calling him Red Bull's next
  Verstappen.
- **Monaco GP**: Antonelli lights to flag, with seven DNFs. Hadjar's first Red Bull podium.
- **Canadian GP**: Russell's PU let go while he led, and he hurled his headrest onto the
  track: "it feels like somebody doesn't want me to fight for this championship."
- **Miami GP**: Chaos. Hulkenberg's Audi caught fire before the sprint, Gasly flipped after
  contact with Lawson, and Antonelli won.
